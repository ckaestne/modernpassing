# Passing with 3+ People


There are many different ways to include more than two passers in a pattern. In this part, we will discuss
**static patterns** where multiple passers stand in specific locations, for example in a triangle, and exchange passes, as well as 
**moving patterns** where some or all passers change their positions during the pattern, usually while they continue to juggle and pass.
In the [next part](6a-intro.md), we will discuss another popular class of group patterns, **manipulator patterns** (also known as takeout patterns), where one or more additional jugglers steal and replace clubs out of a static or moving pattern, often replacing passers in the process.
Patterns can be learned in many orders, but static patterns are usually a good starting point before adding movement or manipulators or both.

**Notation.**
For static and moving pattern, patterns are usually described (a) in terms of the juggling actions of each passer using the notations of [synchronous patterns](2a-intro-notation.md) or [four-handed siteswaps](4a-4hsw-notation.md) and (b) in terms of positions how the passers stand or move, usually diagrams showing positions from above. Although it is not always need, we additionally add an index to the notation of all passes indicating which passer to pass to. Here is an example, of three passers passing 3-count in a triangle:

<sync-group>
A: 3pB333pC33
B: 3pC333pA33
C: 3pA333pB33
positions: A -l B -l C - A
</sync-group>






**Form groups of 3 to 5 passers.** 
Most group patterns are best with 3 or 4 passers, and there are some interesting patterns with 5 passers. Larger groups usually benefit from splitting into smaller groups.
While it can be fun to pass with many people at the same time and there are some patterns that scale to very large numbers of passers, it can be very difficult to keep even simple large-group patterns going. Especially in very large groups of 10 or more passers, the chance that somebody drops early is very high, resulting in frequent resets of patterns that are often not particularly challenging or interesting for each passer individually.
