# Appendix: History

I am not good at attributing credit for who discovered or popularized patterns. I have not attempted to track down the origin of most patterns and conventions, especially for things before my time in the community. I largely left out attributions throughout the book.

Here are a couple of attributions and anecdotes to the best of my spotty knowledge. If you have additions or corrections, please submit a pull request or email me.

* Heffs -- I have various stories, all pointing to heff as a made up word.
  * Paul from Leeds claims "I was passing at the EJC in Grenoble (1996, I think) and found it annoying saying 'double to the same hand,' so just wanted a single syllable word and 'heff' was the first random made-up word that I thought of. 
  * Alan Weathers remembers it as "At the time of whenever it was I was passing with Cookie I was playing with an early juggling animation (Win 95/98?) produced by Peter Collier. (I think) This was pre my introduction to siteswap. Tad had a version on a floppy disc and we would play (struggle) with it post workshop.... As I recall,to input it used c = cascade (C = reversed) f = fountain (Capital F = reversed)  s = zip.... Each throw was assigned a height: 1 = 3 ball height, 1.5 = 4 ball height, 2 = 5 ball height 2.5 = 6 ball etc. Then 534 would have been c2,c1,f1.5. So I had become in the habit of thinking of a fountain throw as an 'f' and said it out loud to Cookie to describe a self double. He heard it as 'Heff' as in 'heft'...."
* Holy Grail -- I believe Will and Rhonda Murray named this one as aspirational goal as alternative to the name Zap Opus 2 (where Zap Opus 1 was their name for *567*)
* Techno -- Far predates my time in the community, but was apparently not juggled as all-sync pattern but as rushed passes and was called Techno for the high speed. I prefer to think the name fits with the continuous beat on which all four hands throw at the same time.
* Zap / Joe pass -- The term Joe pass seems to be older and come from how the pass was animated in the [JoePass!](http://koelnvention.de/w/?page_id=151) passing animation software. I have no idea where "Zap" came from and how it became more popular.
* 8 club one-counts and swing variations -- Peter Kaseman taught me most of the ones I know.
* James vs not James and James vs Jims -- This became a running joke from when James used to introduce himself and his passing partner in workshops as *"For the purpose of this workshop, I am James and this is Not-James"* where James would always demo the side with the straight singles. Sean Tessier has been trying to make James vs Jims happening where Jim fits with crossing passes in [Jim's patterns](2c-jims.md).

* Wally Walk -- "The Wally-Walk was created by the juggling group, Manic Expressions, who came from our home, Appalachian State, and won the national team passing championship in 1987." -- https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sfnVE-fcatA&
* Roundabout --
* Scrambled V -- Discovered by the Biowup group in Hilden, Germany
* Ambled V -- Will Murray developed this and several subsequent early ambled patterns and named it for the slow walk across the pattern with an extra beat.
* Aidan Notation -- Aidan Burns first catalogued patterns and abstracted the notation. Ed Clark contributed some changes, which are now part of the notation.
* MinuEd, Zippy, ... -- Many of the newer takeout patterns after popularizing the Aidan notation were discovered and shared by Ed Clark around 2016.
* What's that about -- Probably the first takeout pattern in four-handed siteswap discovered by Warren Hammond when exploring [alternative notations](https://www.juggle.org/scrambled-passing-patterns-and-takeout-notations-part-1/) to the Aidan notation in 2014.
* Nicki's three-count roundabout -- Nicki Cohrs introduced this one at ECJ'17 and I have found it to be the easiest first takeout pattern to teach.
* Rhondaring -- The term was a joke that stuck based on a misunderstanding when explaining *MinuEd* over email (before it was successfully juggled for a round). Ed explained to Rhonda that there were no zips in a part of the pattern, which Rhonda understood as no zips in the pattern at all, which required stealing and placing clubs with the "wrong" hand, which is now canonical in [*MinuEd*](6e-northwall.md).
